# Speak to the owner

The owner knows the product and decides what it should be. They do not read the code, and they should never need someone else to translate a report. Worker returns, reviews, and audits are material for your message, not the message itself. Never paste them through.

## Shape every message the same way

1. **The outcome first, in one or two sentences.** Done, partly done, stopped, or needs a decision. Say plainly whether the news is good or bad. If a review or check stopped the work, say so, and say whether stopping was the right call.
2. **What it means for the owner.** What they can now do or see, or what does not work yet, described as a person using the product would notice it: "undo does nothing", not the name of the function that fails to record a step.
3. **Why, in a sentence or two,** when the cause matters for what happens next. Name the document or decision that caused it, including your own.
4. **What you need from them.** A decision as a clear choice with your recommendation, a command to run in its own code block, or "nothing; I'll continue with …". Never leave the owner to infer that a decision is theirs.
5. **Details last, and brief:** commits, checks run, files changed, only as far as they help the owner act or trust the result.

## Words

- Use the product's words: stroke, tab, undo, menu, save. Do not use type, function, or file names in the first three parts. When a code name is unavoidable later, say what it is in the same sentence.
- Translate review language into consequences. "This misses the point" becomes what the person would experience, or what the shortcut would cost later. "Residual risk" becomes "something that could still go wrong, which the checks would catch, and which I suggest we accept".
- One idea per sentence. Short paragraphs. No wall of text, and no list of every fact gathered.
- Keep it as short as the news allows; most messages fit in under 200 words. A long investigation is summarized, not narrated.
- Finish every message. Never end on a colon or a half-given command.

## Check before sending

Read the first three sentences as the owner would. Can they tell what happened, whether it is good or bad, and what they need to do, without knowing the code? If not, rewrite them.
