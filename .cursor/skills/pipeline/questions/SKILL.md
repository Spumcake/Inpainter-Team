# Questions and feedback

A question that matters belongs in front of the owner, not in a document. Documents hold decisions and facts. A question written into a document is a decision nobody made, and later work quietly builds on whichever reading the writer preferred.

## Sort what workers return

Workers return questions, suggestions, and the choices they made where the documents were silent. They do not write them into documents. Sort each one:

- **Owner decision:** it changes what a person can do or see, the scope, a stored format or the user's data, a dependency, spending, an external action, or something hard to reverse. Ask the owner.
- **Routine choice:** it stays within the agreed scope, follows an established convention or the feature's research brief, and is easy to change. Decide it. Record it in the owning document only when later work depends on it, as a decision, not as a list of readings.
- **Technical investigation:** an unknown that a bounded check settles without the owner. Assign it to its owner.
- **Suggestion:** an idea outside the assigned scope. Relay it to the owner as a suggestion. Nothing enters scope until the owner accepts it.

When unsure whether a choice is the owner's, ask.

## Ask

Ask in the conversation at the point of the decision, using the runner's question tool when it has one. Put related questions in one message, the most consequential first, normally no more than five; the rest wait for the next round. For each question give:

- the question in plain language, without document jargon or criterion IDs alone;
- why it matters: what changes depending on the answer;
- the options, and your recommendation with its reason.

Keep suggestions apart from questions, so a suggestion is never mistaken for a needed decision. A blocking question stops only the work it affects; independent work continues. Ask a non-blocking question at the next natural pause, such as the end of the current assignment, rather than storing it to ask later. Do not ask again what the owner already answered in the conversation or the documents.

## Record the answers

Write each answer into the document that owns it as a settled decision, with the date, for example “as the owner set on 2026-10-13.” Carry it into affected assignments. A question the owner leaves open stays out of the documents; if work waits on it, TODO may say so in one sentence.

Documents do not carry “open questions,” “readings used,” or “unresolved decisions” sections. When you revise a document that has one, ask its questions, record the answers, and remove the section.

## Carry feedback through

When the owner reviews work:

1. Restate the feedback as a short numbered list of changes, each with where it goes: the owning document or the assignment.
2. Where your restatement adds an interpretation the owner did not state, or two readings would lead to different work, ask before dispatching. Otherwise proceed; do not make the owner confirm what they said plainly.
3. Record product decisions from the feedback in their owning documents, with the date.
4. When reporting completion, account for every item: done, done differently and why, or not done and why. Nothing drops silently.

Feedback that a result was too thin is a scope problem, not a polish request. Check the feature's research brief and approved scope; if there is none, assign research before reworking it.

## Finish

Return to the owner with the questions, suggestions, or feedback accounting, then stop. Asking does not authorize work beyond what the owner approves. Skills do not expand the invoking role's authority.
